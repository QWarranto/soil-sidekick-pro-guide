## Step 1: Open Water Quality Monitoring

1. Navigate to **Water Quality** from the main menu.
2. Your county (selected in Workflow 1) auto-loads relevant EPA monitoring stations.
3. The dashboard shows nearby water bodies and their current quality grades.

```
[VIDEO NARRATION]
"Go to Water Quality in the navigation. The system automatically finds
EPA monitoring stations near your county and shows you real-time grades
for the water bodies closest to your fields."
```

---

## Step 2: Interpret Water Quality Grades

| Grade | Meaning | Your Action |
|-------|---------|-------------|
| A | Excellent — all EPA parameters within guidelines | Continue current practices |
| B | Good — minor elevations in one parameter | Monitor trends |
| C | Fair — some parameters elevated | Review input timing and methods |
| D | Poor — multiple parameters near limits | Adjust practices; consider buffers |
| F | Failing — violations detected | Immediate attention; contact extension office |

### Key contaminants to watch:
- **Nitrate** (MCL: 10 mg/L) — Indicates fertilizer runoff; dangerous for infants.
- **Phosphorus** (no MCL, but ecological concern) — Causes algal blooms.
- **Dissolved Oxygen** — Low levels indicate ecosystem stress.

```
[VIDEO NARRATION]
"Each water body gets a letter grade based on EPA monitoring data. Focus
on nitrate and phosphorus — these are direct indicators of agricultural
runoff. If your nearest stream is showing elevated nitrate, your
fertilizer application timing may need adjustment."

[ON-SCREEN] Highlight the grade card, then expand the contaminant detail panel.
```

## Step 3: Assess Fertilizer Runoff Risk

1. Navigate to **Fertilizer Footprint** from the menu.
2. The system calculates your runoff risk based on:

| Factor | Low Risk | Medium Risk | High Risk |
|--------|----------|-------------|-----------|
| Drainage | Well-drained | Moderate | Poor/clay |
| Water proximity | >5 miles | 1–5 miles | <1 mile |
| Slope | <3% | 3–8% | >8% |
| Organic matter | High | Average | Low |

3. Review the composite risk score and specific risk factors.

```
[VIDEO NARRATION]
"The Fertilizer Footprint tool combines your soil type, slope, and
proximity to water into a single runoff risk score. If you're scoring
medium or high, the system provides specific steps to reduce your impact."
```

---

## Step 4: Review Eco-Friendly Alternatives

1. Scroll to the **Eco-Friendly Alternatives** section.
2. Compare your current inputs against sustainable options:

### Organic Options
- Compost and aged manure
- Fish emulsion and bone meal
- Legume cover crops for nitrogen fixation

### Reduced-Risk Synthetic
- Polymer-coated slow-release fertilizers
- Controlled-release granules
- Split application timing (smaller doses, more frequently)

### Practice Changes
- Buffer strips near water bodies (minimum 35 feet recommended)
- Conservation tillage / no-till
- Cover crops between cash crop seasons
- Precision application (VRT) to reduce total volume

```
[VIDEO NARRATION]
"You don't have to go fully organic to reduce your environmental impact.
Sometimes it's as simple as switching to a slow-release coating on the
same fertilizer, or splitting one big application into two smaller ones.
The system shows you the tradeoffs for each option."
```

---

## Step 5: Generate an Environmental Impact Report

1. Click **Export Environmental Report** (PDF).
2. The report includes:
   - Water quality grades for nearby monitoring stations
   - Your runoff risk assessment
   - Recommended practice changes
   - Compliance documentation for state/federal requirements
3. Save for your records or submit to regulatory agencies.

> 💡 **Tip:** This report can satisfy documentation requirements for USDA conservation programs (EQIP, CSP) and state nutrient management plans.

```
[VIDEO NARRATION]
"Export the Environmental Impact Report as a PDF. This is your
compliance paper trail. If you're applying for USDA conservation
program payments, this report shows you're monitoring and mitigating
your impact."

[TRANSITION] "Now that you understand your soil and environment, let's
plan your season."
```
